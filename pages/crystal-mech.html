About

Crystal Mech is a game based on rogue-likes developed for the first UTS Game Studio. This project has also participated in the June 2020 showcase. Journeying through the deepest caverns, you traverse the crystalline mines taking down enemies as you go. As you defeat robots you can earn weapons with greater power and increased abilities the deeper you traverse. However you'd want to keep yourself on your toes as your robotic enemies become much stronger as you become strong.

Project purpose

The purpose of this project is to develop a high-level project using the skills learnt from the subject. The Game Studio 1 subject taught a lot of important theoretical skills in games development and in software development. Throughout the duration of the entire games development course, the Agile/SCRUM methodology has been partly used to address the overall cycle and project management. This course explores the different specific step by step areas in using SCRUM in our software/games development. Besides the overall premise of the game's result, the overall purpose was in experiencing the use of industry methods in project management and experiencing the benefits and challenges in following a process in software development.

Objective

The objective of this project is to create a fully functioning game that achieves the intended design defined during project planning, sprints or milestones.

Approach

When developing the game, our group has undertaken the extensive use of the SCRUM methodology in managing our game development. Firstly we spent the early period in developing plans and creating a design document and pitch that presents the overall idea and design of the game. From there the project underwent a series of fortnightly sprints and multiple meetings in-between where relevant communications are held in discussing concerns, tasks/user stories and progress of assigned sprints. After a few periods of sprints deliverables are then finalised and a reflection following the Recount-React-Analyse pattern is completed.

My role

Throughout development, each individual has undertaken multiple roles in development to both share the load and interface experiences of each member within that area. However for the most part, I've heavily worked in both programming and artistic elements within development. This often involved developing multiple components for primarily the enemies and weapons. Additionally this has opened the opportunity in developing under the SOLID principles when both discussing and learning with other members of the group.

Secondly, I've worked on some art that was created for both conveying designs of the game and for the cover art of the project. However my role in artistic elements extended over how the artistic design decided will be represented over the visual language and design of the game.

Personal contributions

  • Work on preliminary setup of player systems.
  • Development of improvements on present weapon systems including targeting systems and base weapon classes.
  • Post processing work on Unity's high definition render pipeline.
  • Weapon and experience particle systems.
  • Custom shadergraph shaders for HDRP unlit particles.
  • Modified kitbashed audio files for sound effects done through Audacity.
  • Custom crystal assets created through procedurally generated displacements in Blender.
  • Fine tuned lighting settings and added light probes.
  • Digital art created for both the cover art and the initial concept illustration.
